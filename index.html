Built around your life

Build your business around your life. Not the other way around.

Too many cleaning business owners burn out, physically and emotionally, chasing the industry's standard definition of success: more crews, more revenue, more hours.

But success is personal. Maybe it's picking your kids up from school. Maybe it's never scrubbing another shower. Maybe it's a steady income without the 60-hour weeks.

Decide what you want your life to look like first. Then build the business that supports it.

Ask yourself

  • What does "enough" income look like for me?
  • How many hours do I actually want to work each week?
  • Which parts of the work do I love, and which drain me?
  • What would I do with more time back?

Your answers are your business plan.

Building your team

1099 or W2? There's a right fit for you.

Both models can get you out of the cleaning. They come with different costs, rules, and levels of control. I'll walk you through both.

Model one

1099 subcontractors

Independent cleaners who run their own business and take jobs from yours.

  • Lower overhead, no payroll taxes
  • Easier to start lean and grow as you book
  • Needs a solid subcontractor agreement
  • Less control over how and when the work is done
Model two

W2 employees

A team you hire, train, schedule, and pay through payroll.

  • Full control over training, schedules, and standards
  • Stronger loyalty and a consistent client experience
  • Payroll taxes, workers' comp, and more paperwork
  • Easier to build managers who run things for you

From experience

I've done both. Before Brightside, I handled hiring and onboarding at Dairy Queen: W-4s, I-9s, W-2s, employee handbooks, the works. My take? It's easier to start with 1099 and switch to W2 later than the other way around.

Lauren

Worker classification rules vary by state. Talk with a CPA or attorney before you choose a model.

Common questions

Growing a cleaning business? Start with these.

Honest answers to the questions cleaning business owners ask most.

Do I need a million-dollar cleaning business to be successful?

No. Less than 3% of cleaning businesses ever reach six figures, so a million isn't the right first goal for most owners. Focus on pricing for profit, building recurring clients, and knowing your numbers. A business that pays you well and fits your life is a successful business.

How do I start a cleaning business?

Start small and legal: pick a business name, register it with your state, get general liability insurance, and set your prices before you book anyone. Then land your first few clients through friends, neighbors, and local Facebook groups. Focus on recurring clients from day one, because steady repeat work is what makes a cleaning business stable.

How much should I charge for house cleaning?

Price from your costs, not from what the cheapest company in town charges. Add up labor, supplies, insurance, software, and the profit you want, then work back to a price per visit. My free KPI tracker helps you see your labor cost and profit per job so you know your prices are working.

How do I avoid burnout as a cleaning business owner?

Decide what you want your life to look like first, then build around it. Price so you don't have to overwork, get help with the physical cleaning sooner than you think, and automate the admin so your evenings are yours. Burnout usually comes from chasing someone else's definition of success.

Should I hire 1099 subcontractors or W2 employees?

Both can work. 1099 subcontractors usually mean lower overhead and an easier start, but less control over how the work is done. W2 employees give you full control over training and standards, with payroll taxes and more paperwork. In my experience, it's easier to start with 1099 and switch to W2 later than the other way around. Classification rules vary by state, so check with a CPA or attorney before you decide.

How do I get my first cleaning clients?

Start with people who already know you, then ask every happy client for a referral and a review. Local Facebook groups, Nextdoor, and a Google Business Profile bring in steady leads. Recurring clients are worth far more than one-time cleans, so offer weekly and biweekly service first.
